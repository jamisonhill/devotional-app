# Day 1: "Simon, Simon" — When God Calls You by Name

## The Sifting of Simon Peter — A 7-Day Devotional

---

### Scripture Reading

> "Simon, Simon, behold, Satan demanded to have you, that he might sift you like wheat."
> — Luke 22:31 (ESV)

> "Prepare to meet your God, O Israel!"
> — Amos 4:12 (ESV)

---

### Devotional

Notice something. Jesus does not say, "Peter." He says, "Simon, Simon." The repetition of the name is urgent, tender, and personal. It is the voice of a shepherd who sees the wolf coming before the sheep do.

This is how God deals with His children. He does not let the great trials of life ambush us without any word of preparation. He comes to us beforehand — sometimes through Scripture, sometimes through a brother or sister, sometimes through an unsettling in our spirit — and He says our name. He gets our attention. He tells us something is coming.

Do you see this? Before the sifting, before the denial, before Peter's world caved in — Jesus spoke. He warned. He prepared.

Now, why does this matter? Because many of us live as though the Christian life should be a smooth road from conversion to glory. And when suffering strikes, when temptation overwhelms, when the ground gives way beneath us, we think God was not paying attention. We think He was looking the other way.

But Luke 22:31 will not let us believe that. Jesus knew exactly what was coming for Peter. He knew it before Peter had any idea. And He spoke into it. He did not remove the trial, but He did not leave Peter uninformed either.

There is a profound mercy in this. God does not owe us an explanation before every season of suffering. Job received none. But often — more often than we recognize — God gives us signals. He puts a verse in front of us. He lays a weight on our hearts in prayer. He sends a friend who says just the right thing at just the right time. These are the "Simon, Simon" moments of our lives.

And here is the key: the warning is not given to produce anxiety. It is given to produce alertness. Jesus did not say, "Simon, Simon, you should be terrified." He said, in effect, "Simon, Simon, I see what is coming, and I am not leaving you alone in it." The warning is a form of love.

The prophet Amos cried out to Israel, "Prepare to meet your God!" (Amos 4:12). That sounds severe — and it is. But it is also grace. God calls us to readiness not because He delights in our fear, but because He delights in our faith. And faith that is alert is faith that endures.

So let me ask you today: Is God saying your name? Is He putting something in front of you — a passage, a conviction, a restlessness — that might be His way of saying, "I want you to be ready"? Do not brush it aside. Do not numb yourself to it. The God who calls you by name is the God who keeps you by His power. And the fact that He warns you is proof that He is watching over you.

The shepherd who calls the sheep by name is the shepherd who will lay down His life for them. Jesus proved that — on the same night He spoke these words to Simon.

---

### Reflection Questions

1. Can you recall a time when God seemed to prepare you for a difficult season before it arrived? What did that preparation look like?

2. How do you typically respond when you sense God warning or unsettling you — with anxiety, or with trust? What would it look like to respond with alert faith?

3. What does it mean to you personally that Jesus calls you by name — that He knows what is ahead and speaks into it before it comes?

---

### Prayer

Father, I confess that I often live unaware — unaware of the spiritual realities around me, unaware of Your warnings, unaware of how closely You watch over my life. Open my ears to hear when You call my name. Give me the grace not to be anxious, but to be alert. And let my alertness be rooted not in fear of what is coming, but in confidence that You are already there. In Jesus' name, Amen.
