# Day 3: The Sovereignty of God Over Satan's Sieve

## The Sifting of Simon Peter — A 7-Day Devotional

---

### Scripture Reading

> "Simon, Simon, behold, Satan demanded to have you, that he might sift you like wheat."
> — Luke 22:31 (ESV)

> "And the LORD said to Satan, 'Behold, all that he has is in your hand. Only against him do not stretch out your hand.'"
> — Job 1:12 (ESV)

> "And we know that for those who love God all things work together for good, for those who are called according to his purpose."
> — Romans 8:28 (ESV)

---

### Devotional

There is a word in Luke 22:31 that changes everything. It is the word "demanded." Satan *demanded* to have the disciples. He asked. He requested. He petitioned.

Stop and feel the weight of that. Satan does not have independent authority to do whatever he pleases with the children of God. He must come before the throne and make his case. He must ask permission.

This is precisely the picture we get in Job 1. Satan appears before God, and God initiates the conversation: "Have you considered my servant Job?" And when Satan proposes a test, God sets the boundaries: "Behold, all that he has is in your hand. Only against him do not stretch out your hand" (Job 1:12). Satan operates on a leash. The leash is held by a sovereign and good God.

Now this truth can go one of two directions in your heart. It can trouble you: "If God is sovereign, why does He allow the sifting at all?" Or it can comfort you: "If God is sovereign, then the sifting has limits, and it has purpose."

I want to press you toward the second response, because that is where Scripture leads.

The Father granted Satan the power to sift Simon Peter. But here is what is breathtaking: in response to the prayer of His Son, the Father did not let Simon fall through the sieve. The sifting was real. The pain was real. The failure was real. But the destruction was not. God set a boundary that Satan could not cross.

And what happened inside that boundary? God broke the back of Peter's pride and self-reliance. Peter walked into that night saying, "Lord, I am ready to go with you both to prison and to death" (Luke 22:33). He walked out of it weeping bitterly, shattered, humbled, and — this is the miracle — still held. Still loved. Still called.

Do you see what God was doing? He was using Satan's sieve for His own purposes. Satan meant to destroy Peter's faith. God meant to destroy Peter's self-confidence — which is an entirely different thing. God used the enemy's assault to strip away the one thing that was actually making Peter weak: his trust in himself.

This is Romans 8:28 in action. God does not merely tolerate evil and suffering. He governs it. He bends it. He makes it serve the good of those who love Him and are called according to His purpose.

So when you find yourself in the sieve — shaken, scraped, disoriented — do not conclude that God has lost control. He has not. The sieve is in Satan's hands, but Satan's hands are in God's hands. And God is doing something in you that the comfort of an easy life could never accomplish.

The sovereignty of God does not mean the absence of pain. It means the absence of meaningless pain. Every trial has a purpose. Every sifting has a limit. And every child of God who comes through the sieve comes out more dependent on Christ and less dependent on themselves — which is exactly where God wants us.

---

### Reflection Questions

1. Does the sovereignty of God over your trials comfort you or trouble you? Why? What would it take for you to rest more fully in His governance over your suffering?

2. Is there an area of self-reliance in your life that God might be stripping away through a current difficulty? What would it look like to let Him do that work?

3. How does the picture of Satan on a leash — accountable to God, limited by God — change the way you face spiritual opposition?

---

### Prayer

Sovereign Father, I tremble and I trust. I tremble because the sifting is real and it hurts. I trust because You hold the sieve, You set the limits, and You bend even the enemy's malice to serve Your good purposes in my life. Forgive me for the self-reliance that makes me weak. Break what needs breaking, and hold what needs holding. I want to come through this trial more dependent on Christ than I was before. For Your glory and my joy, Amen.
