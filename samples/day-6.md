# Day 6: "When You Have Turned Back" — Repentance and Restoration

## The Sifting of Simon Peter — A 7-Day Devotional

---

### Scripture Reading

> "And when you have turned again, strengthen your brothers."
> — Luke 22:32b (ESV)

> "And the Lord turned and looked at Peter. And Peter remembered the saying of the Lord, how he had said to him, 'Before the rooster crows today, you will deny me three times.' And he went out and wept bitterly."
> — Luke 22:61-62 (ESV)

> "The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise."
> — Psalm 51:17 (ESV)

> "The steadfast love of the LORD never ceases; his mercies never come to an end; they are new every morning; great is your faithfulness."
> — Lamentations 3:22-23 (ESV)

---

### Devotional

There is one word in Luke 22:32 that carries the entire weight of the gospel: "when."

Jesus does not say, "*If* you turn again." He says, "*When* you have turned again." Before Peter has denied Him — before the rooster has crowed, before the bitter tears have fallen — Jesus speaks of Peter's repentance as a settled certainty. It will happen. Not because Peter is reliable, but because Jesus has prayed.

This is breathtaking. At the very moment Jesus predicts Peter's worst failure, He simultaneously predicts Peter's certain restoration. The fall is coming. And so is the recovery. Both are sure. The fall is sure because Peter is weak. The recovery is sure because Christ is strong.

Now look at the moment of turning. Luke 22:61 gives us one of the most piercing scenes in all of Scripture: "And the Lord turned and looked at Peter." That is all. No words. Just a look. And it broke Peter open.

What was in that look? I do not think it was anger. I do not think it was disappointment. I think it was the look of someone who had said, "I have prayed for you," and meant it. It was a look of sorrow and love so intertwined that Peter could not tell where one ended and the other began. And it sent him out into the night, weeping bitterly.

These are not the tears of a man whose faith has failed. These are the tears of a man whose faith is being reborn. A faithless man does not weep over his denial. A faithless man shrugs, moves on, hardens. But Peter wept — because he still loved Jesus, because he knew what he had done, and because the look of Christ had pierced through every wall he had built.

This is what repentance looks like. It is not a program. It is not a technique. It is the shattering of self-sufficiency and the falling of a broken heart into the hands of a merciful God. "A broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise" (Psalm 51:17). God does not despise Peter's tears. He honors them. He receives them. He meets them with restoration.

And this is the word I want to press into your heart today: if you have failed — if you have denied Christ by your words or your silence, by your choices or your cowardice — the word of Jesus over your life is not "if" but "when." When you turn. When you come back. When you fall broken before Him.

He is not waiting to condemn you. He is waiting to restore you. His mercies are new every morning (Lamentations 3:23). The same Jesus who looked at Peter in that courtyard is looking at you right now — not with contempt, but with the fierce tenderness of a Savior who already prayed for you before you fell.

Come back. Turn again. He will not despise your broken heart. He will heal it.

---

### Reflection Questions

1. Is there a failure or a season of distance from God that you have been carrying with shame? What would it mean to hear Jesus say "when" — not "if" — over your return to Him?

2. What do you think was in the look that Jesus gave Peter? How does that picture of Christ shape your understanding of how He sees you in your failures?

3. What is the difference between worldly sorrow and godly repentance (see 2 Corinthians 7:10)? Which one characterizes your response to sin?

---

### Prayer

Lord Jesus, I come to You with the tears of Peter. I have failed You — in ways others have seen and in ways only You know. And I have carried the weight of it, wondering if You still want me, if You still have a purpose for me. But Your Word says "when," not "if." You are not surprised by my failure, and You are not finished with me. Receive my broken heart. Do not despise it. Restore me by Your mercy — mercy that is new this very morning. I turn to You again. Amen.
