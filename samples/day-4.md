# Day 4: "I Have Prayed for You" — The Intercession of Christ

## The Sifting of Simon Peter — A 7-Day Devotional

---

### Scripture Reading

> "But I have prayed for you that your faith may not fail."
> — Luke 22:32a (ESV)

> "Who is to condemn? Christ Jesus is the one who died — more than that, who was raised — who is at the right hand of God, who indeed is interceding for us."
> — Romans 8:34 (ESV)

> "Consequently, he is able to save to the uttermost those who draw near to God through him, since he always lives to make intercession for them."
> — Hebrews 7:25 (ESV)

---

### Devotional

Of all the sentences in Scripture, this may be one of the most staggering: "I have prayed for you."

These are not the words of a well-meaning friend. These are the words of the eternal Son of God — the one through whom the universe was made, the one who upholds all things by the word of His power, the one who will judge the living and the dead. And He says to a fisherman who is about to fail miserably, "I have prayed for you."

Do you feel the weight of this? The Son of God prays. And He prays for *you*.

Notice the tense. Jesus does not say, "I will pray for you." He says, "I have prayed for you." The Greek indicates a completed action. Before the trial came, the prayer was already offered. Before Peter set foot in that courtyard, before the servant girl asked her question, before the rooster crowed — Jesus had already gone to the Father on Peter's behalf.

And what did He pray? Not that Peter would avoid the sifting. Not that the night would be easy. He prayed "that your faith may not fail." There it is again — faith. Faith is the issue. Faith is the target. And faith is what Jesus prays to preserve.

This is not a one-time event. Romans 8:34 tells us that the risen Christ is, right now, at the right hand of God interceding for us. Hebrews 7:25 says He *always lives* to make intercession. This is His ongoing, unceasing ministry. The same Jesus who prayed for Peter in the upper room is praying for you at this very moment.

And here is why this changes everything: His prayers do not fail. When you and I pray, we pray with faltering faith, with mixed motives, with limited understanding. But when Jesus prays, He prays with perfect knowledge of what we need, perfect alignment with the Father's will, and perfect authority to secure what He asks for.

This means your perseverance does not ultimately depend on the strength of your grip on Christ. It depends on the strength of His grip on you. And His grip is the grip of the one who prays with all authority in heaven and on earth.

Peter denied Jesus three times that night. Three times. And yet his faith did not fail — not ultimately, not finally. Why? Because Jesus had prayed. The denial was real. The tears were real. But the destruction was not, because the prayer of the Son of God stood between Peter and the abyss.

Christian, hear this: you will have seasons when your faith feels thin, when your grip feels weak, when you wonder if you will make it through. In those moments, remember that your survival does not hang on your prayers alone. It hangs on His. And He always lives to intercede.

You are not held in place by the strength of your own devotion. You are held in place by the prayer of the risen Christ. And that prayer will never, ever fail.

---

### Reflection Questions

1. How does it change your experience of spiritual struggle to know that Jesus is actively interceding for you — right now, at the Father's right hand?

2. Have you been through a season where your faith felt paper-thin, and yet you did not abandon Christ? Looking back, can you see the hand of Christ holding you through His intercession?

3. What is the difference between persevering by the strength of your own devotion and persevering because Christ prays for you? How does this distinction affect the way you face today?

---

### Prayer

Lord Jesus, I am undone by this truth — that You pray for me. Not because I deserve it, but because You love me. I confess that I have often treated my perseverance as my own achievement, my own effort, my own white-knuckled determination. But it is Your prayer that holds me. It is Your intercession that keeps my faith from failing utterly. Thank You. I rest in Your grip today — not in my own. You who always live to intercede, intercede for me now. Amen.
