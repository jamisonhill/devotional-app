# Day 7: "Strengthen Your Brothers" — Suffering Turned to Service

## The Sifting of Simon Peter — A 7-Day Devotional

---

### Scripture Reading

> "And when you have turned again, strengthen your brothers."
> — Luke 22:32b (ESV)

> "Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our affliction, so that we may be able to comfort those who are in any affliction, with the comfort with which we ourselves are comforted by God."
> — 2 Corinthians 1:3-4 (ESV)

> "Brothers, if anyone is caught in any transgression, you who are spiritual should restore him in a spirit of gentleness. Keep watch on yourself, lest you too be tempted. Bear one another's burdens, and so fulfill the law of Christ."
> — Galatians 6:1-2 (ESV)

---

### Devotional

Here, at the end of everything — after the warning, after the sifting, after the denial and the tears and the restoration — Jesus reveals the purpose of it all. He does not say, "When you have turned again, go and hide." He does not say, "When you have turned again, retire in shame." He says, "Strengthen your brothers."

The sifting was not pointless suffering. It was preparation for ministry.

Do you see the logic of heaven here? Jesus provided for the other ten disciples — the ones Satan also wanted to sift — by strengthening Peter first. The strengthened becomes the strengthener. The one who has been broken and restored becomes the very instrument God uses to hold others together.

This is the economy of God's grace: nothing is wasted. Not the failure. Not the shame. Not the bitter tears in the courtyard. All of it — every jagged edge of the sieve — becomes raw material for ministry. Peter could not have written, "Cast all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you" (1 Peter 5:7) with the same authority if he had never been in the sieve himself. He could not have warned, "Be sober-minded; be watchful" (1 Peter 5:8) if he had not once been drunk on self-confidence and blind to the prowling lion.

Paul says the same thing in 2 Corinthians 1:3-4. God comforts us in our affliction *so that* we may comfort others. The "so that" is the hinge. Our suffering is not the end of the story. It is the preparation for the next chapter — a chapter in which we become the hands and voice of God to someone else who is being sifted.

And notice the tenderness that Galatians 6:1 requires. "You who are spiritual should restore him in a spirit of gentleness. Keep watch on yourself, lest you too be tempted." Who can do this? Not the person who has never failed. That person restores with a hammer. The person who can restore with gentleness is the one who remembers the courtyard, who remembers the look of Jesus, who remembers what it felt like to be held when they deserved to be dropped.

This is why I say: eternal security is a community project. God does not merely save us as isolated individuals. He saves us into a body. And He designs our trials so that our survival becomes the means of someone else's survival. Peter's sifting produced Peter's ministry. And Peter's ministry produced the strengthening of the church.

So here is my final word to you in this series: your trial is not just about you. Your sifting — the one you are in right now, or the one you have come through — has a purpose beyond your own soul. God is making you into a strengthener. He is turning your suffering into service.

Do not hoard the comfort you have received. Do not bury the lessons the sieve has taught you. There is someone in your life — in your church, in your family, in your circle — who is being shaken right now. They need someone who has been there. They need someone who can say, "I know what it feels like to nearly let go — and I know the One who held me fast."

Be that person. Strengthen your brothers. This is why you were sifted.

---

### Reflection Questions

1. How has your own experience of suffering, failure, or spiritual struggle equipped you to minister to others in ways you could not have before?

2. Is there someone in your life right now who is being "sifted" — who needs the comfort and strength that God has given you through your own trials? What would it look like to come alongside them this week?

3. What does it mean to you that "eternal security is a community project"? How does this shape the way you think about your role in your church or Christian community?

---

### Prayer

Father of mercies and God of all comfort, thank You for the sieve. I did not want it. I would not have chosen it. But You used it to break my pride, deepen my dependence on Christ, and prepare me to serve others. Now give me eyes to see the brothers and sisters around me who are being shaken. Give me the courage to come alongside them — not with easy answers, but with the real comfort I have received from You. Make me a strengthener. Let nothing You have done in my life be wasted. For the glory of Christ and the building up of His church, Amen.
