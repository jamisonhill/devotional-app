# Day 2: Satan Is Real and He Wants Your Faith

## The Sifting of Simon Peter — A 7-Day Devotional

---

### Scripture Reading

> "Simon, Simon, behold, Satan demanded to have you, that he might sift you like wheat."
> — Luke 22:31 (ESV)

> "Be sober-minded; be watchful. Your adversary the devil prowls around like a roaring lion, seeking someone to devour."
> — 1 Peter 5:8 (ESV)

> "For we do not wrestle against flesh and blood, but against the rulers, against the authorities, against the cosmic powers over this present darkness, against the spiritual forces of evil in the heavenly places."
> — Ephesians 6:12 (ESV)

---

### Devotional

Let us be blunt: Satan is real. He is not a metaphor. He is not a symbol for the dark side of human nature. He is a person — a fallen, malicious, intelligent being — and he has an agenda for your life.

Jesus did not say, "Simon, you are going to struggle with some inner doubts." He said, "Satan demanded to have you." This is a personal enemy with a personal aim. And if we miss this, we will misdiagnose almost everything that happens to us in the Christian life.

Now here is what I want you to see — and this is critical. What does Satan want? He does not ultimately want your health. He does not ultimately want your marriage. He does not ultimately want your job or your reputation. He wants your faith. That is the target. Everything else is a means to that end.

Picture it this way: Satan has a great sieve with jagged-edged wires forming a mesh, and the holes in that mesh are shaped like faithless men and women. What he aims to do is throw you into this sieve and shake you over those jagged edges until you are so torn, so weak, so desperate that you let go of your faith and fall through as a faithless person — right into his company.

Do you see the implication? Faith cannot fit through the sieve. Only faithlessness can. As long as you hold fast to the goodness and power and promises of God, you cannot fall through. Satan knows this. That is why he targets faith and nothing else with ultimate seriousness.

This is why Peter himself — decades later, having lived through the sifting — wrote those words in 1 Peter 5:8. He knew. He had been in the sieve. He had felt the jagged edges. He had nearly let go. And so he warns us: Be sober-minded. Be watchful. Your adversary is real, and he is hunting.

But notice: Peter does not say, "Be terrified." He says, "Be watchful." There is a massive difference. Terror paralyzes. Watchfulness mobilizes. The Christian who knows that Satan is real and that faith is the target does not cower — he prays. He takes up the shield of faith (Ephesians 6:16). He fights not with fists but with trust.

If Satan can devour your faith, he does not care what else you do. You can be moral, respectable, religious — even churched — and if faith is gone, Satan has what he wanted. But if you hold fast to Christ, if you keep trusting the promises of God even when the sieve is shaking violently, then Satan gets nothing. Nothing.

So the question for you today is not whether you are comfortable. It is whether you are trusting. Are you holding fast to Christ in the midst of whatever trial you face? That is the one thing the enemy cannot tolerate — and it is the one thing Jesus died to preserve in you.

---

### Reflection Questions

1. In what area of your life do you sense your faith being most tested right now? Can you see how the trial might be aimed at getting you to stop trusting God?

2. How does understanding that Satan's primary target is your faith — not your circumstances — change the way you interpret your current struggles?

3. What does it look like practically for you to "be sober-minded" and "be watchful" this week?

### Prayer

Lord Jesus, I confess that I often live as though the spiritual battle is not real — as though my struggles are merely human, merely circumstantial. Open my eyes to see what is truly at stake. My faith is precious because it connects me to You. Guard it. Strengthen it. Help me to be watchful without being fearful, and to fight not with my own strength but by holding fast to Your promises. You are my shield. I trust You. Amen.
